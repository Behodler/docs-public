# Antimatter, Explained

*An introduction to Phoenix's latest feature — what it is and how it works.*

## Some quick background

phUSD is backed in two ways: liquidity sitting in AMM pools (POL — protocol owned liquidity) and capital the protocol itself owns (POC — protocol owned capital). POC is what generates protocol derived yield (PDY), and PDY is what funds the rewards you earn by staking phUSD in Phlimbo — which is what makes holding and staking phUSD attractive with market-leading rates of yield.

Separately, the protocol runs a vault where people can stake stablecoins (USDC, DOLA, USDe) and earn a yield on them. That staked capital belongs to the people who put it there and it's free to come and go as they please. It's a great source of activity for the protocol, but because it isn't the protocol's own capital, it doesn't count toward POC.

Antimatter is a new token designed to give stakers a way to turn some of that yield into a bigger, permanent boost for the protocol's own capital base — while getting a better payout for themselves in the process.

## What Antimatter is

Antimatter is a new ERC20 token. It does one thing: **annihilation**. Burn 1 Antimatter together with 1 unit of an accepted stablecoin, and you receive **2 phUSD** in return. The stablecoin you contribute goes into the protocol's POC pool.

The name is inspired by the physics concept it mirrors: matter meets antimatter and the two annihilate each other, releasing energy. Here, your stablecoin meets Antimatter, the pair is annihilated, and what's released is phUSD.

Instead of the stablecoin staking vault paying out phUSD directly, it will pay out Antimatter. When you claim your rewards, you hold Antimatter tokens rather than phUSD. You then choose when to annihilate — pairing your Antimatter with a stablecoin of your own to mint phUSD, at a rate of 2 phUSD for every 1 Antimatter you burn.

(Worth noting: Phlimbo, Phoenix's main staking pool, is a different product — there you stake phUSD and earn USDC. Antimatter comes from the separate stablecoin-staking vault.)

## What's the benefit?

Every time someone annihilates, the stablecoin they pair with their Antimatter becomes protocol owned capital — permanently. That directly grows POC, and POC is what generates PDY (protocol derived yield). Since PDY is what funds the USDC rewards paid out in Phlimbo, growing POC means increased APY for everyone staking phUSD — not just for the person who annihilated.

Increased PDY also makes it more attractive to buy and hold phUSD. That extra demand helps push price back toward Phoenix's price attractor when it drifts below — what the protocol calls the Rebound Effect — so the benefit doesn't stop at yield, it ripples into price stability too. So the same claim that pays the Antimatter holder more also leaves the whole system stronger than it was before.

The core idea: Antimatter turns an everyday staking reward into an action that benefits the staker and the protocol at the same time.

## How to use:

Once you annihilate your Antimatter, you're left holding phUSD — and from there you have two straightforward options:

- Stake it in Phlimbo to earn ongoing USDC rewards, if you'd rather keep compounding your position.
- Sell it in the Balancer pool for stablecoins right away, if you'd rather cash out immediately.

## Where can I get antimatter?

The stablecoin staking pools will begin rewarding antimatter instead of phUSD. Instead of claiming, stakers will annihilate accumulated antimatter with staked principal, instantly producing large phUSD payouts while growing POC
